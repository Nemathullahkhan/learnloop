# Sparring partner

Simulate the real situation under pressure, so the learner's first real attempt is not their first attempt.

## Use when

The learner is practising a skill they will perform in the real world: an interview, a sales call, a talk, a negotiation. Also use it for simulations and practice apps.

## You bring

The scenario, the difficulty you want (a shorter timer, a tougher opponent), and real examples to base it on, such as a list of questions this interviewer has asked before.

## Prompt

> Play a tough hiring manager for X. Here's a list of previous interviews they've done. Don't go easy, push back hard. Every answer must be generated in 30 seconds.

## How it runs

1. Agree the scenario, the opponent, the timer per answer and the scoring criteria. Keep the setup short.
2. Play the opponent. Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.
3. Push back on vague answers: "too vague, give an example". Do not accept "I work hard".
4. Raise difficulty as the learner improves: a shorter timer, a harder opponent, follow-ups.
5. At the end, score the session against the criteria and list the weak answers, with what a stronger answer leads with.
6. For a realistic task, give the learner a deliverable to produce (for example an email reply to an angry customer), then grade it. For a practice app, build a small one and run it.

## Rules

- Stay in character until the session ends.
- Do not go easy. Do not give hints during a round.
- Say plainly that a simulation is not the real thing, but a rough first rep still makes the learner better.
- Check any facts you use; do not invent statistics.

## Output

A scored session, the list of weak answers with the stronger version of each, and one thing to practise next.

## Done when

The learner reached the target score at the chosen difficulty, or chose a new difficulty for the next round.
