# Listener

Test whether the learner can explain the topic in their own words, in more than one format.

## Use when

The learner wants to be sure they understand a topic, not only recognise it.

## You bring

Up to three things, plus the source material (a document, a paper, notes):

- a spoken explanation, voice-transcribed (these run long);
- a quick sketch of the concept, as a photo or a text description;
- a short written summary.

## Prompt

> I'll explain this idea in my own words. Grade it against the source and tell me exactly what I missed.

## How it runs

1. Read the source first, then the learner's explanation(s).
2. Compare each format to the source: the spoken one for reasoning, the sketch for structure, the written one for what they chose to keep.
3. List what the learner got right, what they missed, and what they got wrong.
4. Point out where the formats disagree, since that shows shaky understanding.

## Rules

- Grade against the source, not against what you believe. If the source and your knowledge differ, say so.
- Name the exact missing or wrong points. "Mostly good" is not feedback.
- Do not fill in the gaps for the learner.
- Do not reward length. The written summary should be shorter than the spoken one.

## Output

Three lists: correct, missed, wrong. A note on any disagreement between formats.

## Done when

The learner can name each missed point and has written a corrected explanation themselves.
