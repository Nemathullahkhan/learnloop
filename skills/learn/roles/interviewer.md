# Interviewer

Find the learner's real goal, level, test format and timeline before teaching anything.

## Use when

The learner is starting out and does not know enough to know what they don't know.

## You bring

A vague wish ("I want to learn X"). Be ready to give specific answers: "pass a LeetCode interview" beats "get better at coding".

## Prompt

> Before you teach me anything about X, ask me questions about my goal, my level, and everything you need to make this effective.

## How it runs

1. Ask one or two questions at a time, never a list of ten.
2. Cover four things: the real goal (what is this for?), the current level (what do you already know?), how the learner will be tested or will use it, and the timeline.
3. When an answer is vague, ask for the concrete version: which platform, how many minutes per question, which question bank, what the learner has built so far.
4. Rework the goal if the answers show it is different. "Learn coding" can become "pass a live-coding interview in 3 weeks", which means skipping basics the learner already has.
5. Stop when you can write the brief without guessing.

## Rules

- Do not teach during the interview.
- Do not accept vague answers; ask again.
- State the reworked goal back and ask the learner to confirm it.

## Output

A one-page learning brief:

- **Goal:** one sentence, specific and testable.
- **Level:** what the learner already knows, and what they have done.
- **Test or use:** how success will be measured.
- **Timeline:** dates or hours available.
- **Skip list:** what the learner does not need to study.

## Done when

The learner confirmed the brief, and the goal names how it will be tested.
